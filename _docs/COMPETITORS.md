# Island Survival Game — Competitive Landscape

**Working title:** Marooned / Island Survivor  
**Status:** Playable Three.js prototype (not a store build)  
**Research date:** 2026-09-02

> **Note:** The naming discussion is intentionally omitted from this document. The current working title can remain **Marooned** internally.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The competitive research is encouraging.

The broad **survival / crafting / building** market is highly competitive, but the exact combination proposed here is much less crowded:

- tropical island setting
- low-poly / friendly visual presentation
- meaningful location-based scavenging
- hunger / thirst / wetness / temperature
- persistent home-building
- shared island
- visible other players
- **no PvP at launch**
- resource races around wrecks and world events

The most important commercial references are:

1. **Raft** — proves that accessible survival + building + multiplayer can become a very large indie hit.
2. **Stranded Deep** — closest mechanical reference for tropical survival.
3. **Project Castaway** — very close contemporary competitor and therefore especially important to watch.
4. **Grounded** — proves stylized/friendly survival can reach a large mainstream audience.
5. **Smalland** — demonstrates that a stylized survival game can work with multiplayer and building.
6. **Survival: Fountain of Youth** — validates the shipwrecked-island + survival + shelter fantasy.
7. **The Survivalists** — useful reference for accessible survival, exploration and co-op.
8. **Among Trees** — useful primarily as an aesthetic / tone reference.

The opportunity is not to beat these games at "more survival mechanics."

It is to make a **simpler, prettier, more immediately readable survival game with a distinctive multiplayer tension loop**.

# 2. The competitive landscape

## 2.1 Raft

![Raft](https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/648800/header.jpg)

**Steam:** https://store.steampowered.com/app/648800/Raft/

### Why it matters

Raft is the strongest commercial proof that the general fantasy works.

Players:

- start with almost nothing
- manage hunger and thirst
- gather resources
- craft tools
- explore islands
- build a home
- play alone or with friends

The critical difference is that the **raft itself** is the main progression object.

For our game, that role is played by the **house / island camp**.

### Commercial scale

Third-party estimates have placed Raft at roughly:

- **~15.8M Steam copies**
- **~$228M estimated gross revenue**

These are estimates, not audited financial figures.

### What to take

**Steal:**

- extremely clear survival premise
- simple first minutes
- visible base progression
- co-op
- strong YouTube / streamer potential
- resource gathering that directly feeds construction

**Leave:**

- floating-raft identity
- shark combat
- endless-ocean progression
- heavy combat / weapons

### Relevance to our game

**Very high.**

Raft proves that "start with nothing → gather → survive → build a home" is a powerful commercial loop.

# 3. Stranded Deep

**Steam:** https://store.steampowered.com/app/313120/Stranded_Deep/

### Why it matters

This is probably the closest mechanical reference.

It has:

- tropical islands
- hunger
- thirst
- temperature / exposure
- crafting
- shelters
- fires
- shipwrecks
- exploration
- survival resources

The important observation is that **the fantasy itself is already validated**.

### Commercial scale

Third-party estimates vary considerably, but commonly place it around:

- **~1.9–3.4M copies**
- **~$22–41M estimated gross**

Again, these are external estimates rather than developer-reported revenue.

### What to take

**Steal:**

- body-survival mechanics
- tropical environment
- shipwreck scavenging
- meaningful environmental resources
- shelter progression

**Leave:**

- relatively serious / realistic presentation
- complexity where it does not create fun
- isolated single-player feeling

### Relevance to our game

**Extremely high.**

This is the game to study when designing the survival systems.

# 4. Project Castaway

![Project Castaway](https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/1713350/header.jpg)

**Steam:** https://store.steampowered.com/app/1713350/Project_Castaway/

### Why it matters

This is probably the competitor I would watch most closely.

It is a contemporary Pacific-island survival crafting game with:

- stranded-on-an-island premise
- resource gathering
- food
- shelter
- weather
- shipwrecks
- exploration
- base building
- online co-op

It entered Early Access in September 2024 and reached full release on **March 17, 2026**.

### Current reception

Steam currently shows approximately:

- **~1,780 reviews**
- **~64–65% positive overall**
- recent reviews around **40–50% positive**

That is a useful warning:

> Having the right ingredients is not enough.

### What this means for our project

Project Castaway demonstrates that the market is willing to accept the fantasy, but also shows the danger of producing another generic first-person survival-crafting game.

Our differentiation should therefore be obvious from the first screenshot / trailer:

**low-poly beauty + readable survival + shared island + no PvP + wreck races.**

# 10. Mobile competition

The mobile market contains a huge number of generic island-survival games.

One example is **Survival Island: Craft & Build**, which has reached **10M+ Android downloads**.

The broad mobile formula is usually:

> island → gather → craft → build → survive

but often adds:

- aggressive advertising
- energy systems
- combat
- zombies / dinosaurs
- grind
- generic graphics
- IAP progression

This is both competition and validation.

### The opportunity

Our product can occupy a much cleaner position:

> **A premium-quality island survival game without the mobile-survival bullshit.**

Potential positioning:

- no pay-to-win
- no energy system
- no loot-box economy
- no "wait 4 hours for your hut"
- no forced combat
- no aggressive ads
- optional cosmetics / Academy content

# 12. The actual differentiation

The strongest idea in the design is not "island survival."

It is:

## Shared island + no PvP + scarce world loot

Imagine:

> You see smoke on the horizon.

A storm has washed a wreck onto the beach.

You and another player both see it.

You run.

They run.

Neither of you can shoot the other.

The winner gets the rare crate.

The loser still gets something else nearby.

Then you return to your camp, dry your clothes, cook your fish and continue building your house.

That is **multiplayer tension without combat**.

It gives us:

- streamer stories
- social interaction
- competition
- scarcity
- emergent moments
- reasons to explore
- reasons to return

without requiring a full PvP game.

# 13. Positioning

I would avoid positioning the game as:

> "DayZ but on a tropical island."

That creates the wrong expectation.

I would also avoid:

> "A cozy survival game."

That makes the survival mechanics sound shallow.

A stronger description is:

> **A beautiful multiplayer island survival game where everyone shares one island — and every valuable wreck is a race.**

Then explain:

> Start with nothing. Find food and water. Stay warm and dry. Scavenge the island. Build your shelter. Survive storms. Race other survivors to valuable wrecks. No PvP.

This is much more distinctive.

# 14. What the competition suggests we should NOT build

The research reinforces the existing MVP philosophy.

Do **not** start with:

- huge open world
- dozens of biomes
- hundreds of crafting recipes
- weapons
- PvP
- zombies
- dinosaurs
- skill trees
- RPG equipment tiers
- complicated illnesses
- farming simulation
- vehicles
- huge character customization
- complicated economy
- mobile IAP
- education systems
- subscription infrastructure

The first playable loop should remain:

```text
Wake on beach
      ↓
Need water
      ↓
Find coconut
      ↓
Explore
      ↓
Find basic materials
      ↓
Rain starts
      ↓
Get wet
      ↓
Build fire
      ↓
Dry yourself
      ↓
See wreck event
      ↓
Race another player
      ↓
Loot / lose
      ↓
Return to camp
      ↓
Build lean-to
      ↓
"I want to make it better tomorrow"
```

If this loop is not fun, additional content will not fix the game.

# 15. Commercial benchmark

The competitor data suggests three useful levels.

### Level 1 — niche indie survival

Examples:

- Among Trees
- Survival: Fountain of Youth
- The Survivalists

**Potential:** millions in gross revenue are possible, but discoverability and retention are difficult.

### Level 2 — successful stylized survival

Examples:

- Smalland
- Grounded

**Potential:** tens of millions in gross revenue become realistic if the game has a strong visual identity and excellent multiplayer / retention.

### Level 3 — breakout survival hit

Example:

- Raft

**Potential:** tens to hundreds of millions in gross revenue.

Obviously this is the exception, not the baseline.

---

# 16. The commercial thesis

The game should not try to win by being:

> "the biggest survival game."

It should try to win by being:

> **the easiest survival game to understand and the nicest survival game to watch.**

The player should understand the game from a single screenshot:

**beautiful island + little survivor + camp + wreck + other people.**

The trailer should communicate the entire loop in under 30 seconds:

> **Wake up → survive → see wreck → race → build home.**

That is potentially a much better indie proposition than attempting to compete directly with the complexity of DayZ, Rust, Sons of the Forest or Valheim.

# 18. Final conclusion

There is competition.

But I don't see a single obvious game that owns this exact proposition:

> **Low-poly tropical island + serious survival needs + persistent home + shared island + meaningful scarce loot + visible players + no PvP.**

That is the space worth testing.

The biggest risk is **not competition**.

The biggest risk is making the game feel like a generic survival-crafting checklist.

The prototype therefore needs to prove one thing above everything else:

## Is it genuinely exciting to see another player running toward the same wreck?

If the answer is **yes**, the concept has something distinctive.

If the answer is **no**, the multiplayer layer is probably not strong enough and we should redesign before investing in a persistent server, mobile version, education system or monetization.
